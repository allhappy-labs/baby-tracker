Home screen — three directions

Same spec, same data, same three trackers. What differs is what the screen makes easy: reading one number in the dark, reading the shape of the whole day, or hitting a target without looking. Dark mode shown, since night feeds are peak usage.

Night Shift

One question answered enormously: how long since the last feed. Everything else recedes. Amber on deep ink, because amber is the kindest accent to dark-adapted eyes.

A — what you gain and give up

Gain: the single most-asked question is legible across a dark room without your glasses. The dial arc is elapsed time only — it never points at a target, because a ring that fills toward "next feed" is a prediction, and the spec rules predictions out.
  • Feed is privileged; sleep and diaper are demoted to rows
  • Dial costs ~250pt of vertical space for one number
  • Reflows cleanly at AX5: dial shrinks, rows stack

B — what you gain and give up

Gain: rhythm. You see cluster feeding, a short nap, a long stretch — the things that actually tell you how the day went — without opening History.
  • Densest of the three; hardest to read half-asleep
  • The ribbon is real work to build and to make accessible
  • Duplicates History, which may make that tab redundant

C — what you gain and give up

Gain: the largest possible tap targets and the shortest path from intent to logged. Status and action are the same object, so there's nothing to aim at twice.
  • Least information per screen of the three
  • Card-as-button needs a clear press state to not feel ambiguous
  • Scales awkwardly if pumping or growth are added later
