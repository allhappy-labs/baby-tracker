App icon — four directions

Real SVG, drawn on the 1024×1024 canvas Icon Composer expects, in discrete layers. No blur, no drop shadow, no glass — Icon Composer applies all of that, plus the squircle mask and the light/dark/tinted/clear variants. Each option is shown large, at roughly Home Screen size, at Settings size, and flattened to monochrome, because tinted mode strips colour entirely — if the mark does not survive as a silhouette, it does not survive.

A crescent opening upward, holding a single drop. Reads three ways at once — moon, cradle, and the feed it's holding — without drawing any of them literally.

How each survives the hard cases

A · CradleHolds up small. Two shapes, high contrast, unambiguous silhouette. The drop-in-crescent reads as a single object rather than two stacked ones. Closest to the app's own vocabulary without repeating an icon from inside it.
B · ArcMost distinctive, least literal. Nothing else on a home screen looks like it, and it ties directly to the running-timer ring. Risk: an open ring with a dot is also just… a ring. Tells you nothing about what the app does until you've opened it once.
C · MoonriseReads fastest. The horizon bars add stability and a hint of "record". But a crescent/moon is the single most common motif in sleep and baby apps — this is the option most likely to look like something you've already seen.
D · Three BeatsWeakest at size. Three dots on an arc collapse into a smear at Settings size, and the concept (three trackers, one rhythm) is invisible without explanation. Included because the idea is right even if the execution isn't.
