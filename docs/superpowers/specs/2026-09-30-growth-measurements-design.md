# Growth measurements and WHO insights

Date: 2026-09-30
Status: approved in conversation, including the 2026-09-30 deletion clarification

## Intent and scope

Parents can record a child's growth during ordinary care logging and see the trend without interpreting a single measurement as a trend. The first release supports weight, length/height, and head circumference. A check-in is one dated record containing any nonempty combination of those measurements. There are no custom measurement types.

Insights gains one Growth card with a weight mini-chart and an **Open growth details** button below the chart. It has no Add button at the top. The card opens a dedicated Growth screen with a chart and history for each recorded measurement type. New check-ins start from **Measure** in Home's logging area beside Sleep, Diaper, and Supplements. Parents can edit or delete existing check-ins from Growth details. The approved visual prototype uses the app's existing dark palette and card design; it is a layout reference, not evidence of an installed feature.

For each measurement type independently: zero readings show an empty state, one reading shows the value and any available percentile without a chart, and two or more readings show a time-series chart. A check-in with weight and length adds one point to each of those two series. The Insights card follows the same rule for its weight mini-chart. Growth details show the latest value, its WHO percentile when available, and the change from the previous reading of that type. Percentile comparisons are descriptive; the app does not diagnose growth problems or issue medical alerts.

## Components and data flow

1. **Growth profile:** A separate per-child record holds the selected WHO reference sex (`girls` or `boys`) and an optional expected due date. New-baby creation requires the reference choice. Existing babies are not assigned a guessed value: their records stay usable, and the app asks for a reference choice before showing percentiles. The profile is editable in baby settings. Keeping it separate from the existing baby sync payload prevents an older client that edits a baby's name from erasing growth metadata.
2. **Measurement check-in:** A local SwiftData model holds a stable ID, child/realm ID, measured timestamp and time-zone ID, immutable logging timestamp, optional weight in kilograms, optional length/height in centimeters, optional head circumference in centimeters, and the measurement position (`lying` or `standing`) when length/height is present. It follows existing tombstone, modification-time, and sync-outbox conventions. A single check-in is the unit of create, edit, and delete.
3. **Growth reference calculator:** A small, pure client-side component consumes a measurement, child birth date, growth profile, and versioned bundled WHO reference tables. It returns either percentile and reference-curve values or a specific unavailability reason. It never persists calculated percentiles or sends them to the server. Updating birth date, reference sex, due date, or app reference tables recalculates the display.
4. **Growth presentation:** The Home sheet validates and saves check-ins through BabyStore. Insights and Growth details read the same stored records and calculator output. Growth details can select weight, length/height, or head circumference; it does not offer a second creation route. The chart plots recorded values against measurement date and displays WHO reference guidance, including a median line and a 3rd–97th percentile band when percentile data is available. Chart accessibility exposes dated values and percentiles in text.
5. **Family sync and archive:** Add versioned sync payloads for growth profile and measurement check-ins, server storage/migration, snapshot/journal handling, child deletion cascade, and owner export. Sync raw records and profile fields only. A capability boundary must reject older clients before they advance a cursor past a growth entity, including paginated sync and snapshot continuations. Existing clients can resume after updating. Local-only families continue to work offline.

## Measurement and reference rules

- The entry sheet defaults to the current date/time and supports backdating, but rejects a future measured time or one before the child's birth date. Multiple check-ins on one day are allowed and remain separate; sort by measured timestamp, then stable ID. Save requires at least one measurement. Entered values must be finite and positive; invalid fields receive specific errors rather than being silently changed. All stored values use kilograms and centimeters; the first release's entry and display units are kg and cm.
- Length/height asks whether the child was measured lying down or standing, with the age-appropriate choice preselected. The original number and method are retained. The WHO length-for-age reference uses recumbent length before 24 months and standing height from 24 months; the calculator applies WHO's 0.7 cm position adjustment only for comparison when the recorded method differs. The recorded value displayed to parents is never changed.
- Use the sex-specific WHO weight-for-age, length/height-for-age, and head-circumference-for-age standards from birth through 60 months. Bundle the required reference parameters with source/version attribution and calculate percentiles on device, including interpolation needed for the measurement's age in days. Check implementation results against published WHO values, especially birth, the 24-month length/height transition, and the 60-month boundary. See [WHO standards](https://www.who.int/tools/child-growth-standards/standards) and [WHO length/height reference](https://www.who.int/toolkits/child-growth-standards/standards/length-height-for-age).
- Interpret the measured timestamp in its stored time zone to obtain the measurement's calendar date. Compute age from that date and the child's date-only birth/due dates, so travel or a different viewer time zone cannot shift a percentile. When an expected due date is later than the birth date, use `measurement date − expected due date` as corrected age until the child's third chronological birthday; then use age from birth. Without a later due date, use age from birth. Readings before the due date or outside the WHO 0–60-month reference remain visible without a percentile. If a later edit to the birth date makes an older reading pre-birth, preserve it but hide its percentile until corrected. This follows the [AAP's corrected-age guidance](https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/newborn-infant-and-early-childhood-nutrition/newborn-and-infant-nutrition-assessment-tools/term-infant-growth-tools/). The first release does not bundle preterm-specific Fenton curves.
- A percentile can be shown for one reading, but a chart needs two readings **of that measurement type**. The card and charts never invent intermediate observations. A missing profile, unavailable reference row, or invalid input suppresses only the affected calculated insight; raw readings remain visible. The UI labels the reference and corrected-age use, and avoids interpreting percentile rank as a health verdict.

## Sync, migration, and failure behavior

- A new versioned SwiftData schema adds the two growth models while allowing an existing baby to have no growth profile. Migration preserves existing babies and records without guessing reference sex. New-baby creation saves the baby and required local growth profile together. If remote mutations arrive in separate sync pages, the UI tolerates a baby whose profile has not arrived yet.
- Server validation requires a child in the same family, a nonempty check-in, finite positive values, a valid position when length/height exists, and consistent date/time fields. It preserves the canonical measurement timestamp and immutable logging timestamp under the existing sync rules. Family permissions, conflict/version rules, tombstones, snapshots, and export follow the other care records.
- The server advertises a growth capability. Once a growth profile or check-in exists in a family, clients without that capability receive the existing unsupported-capability update path before any response advances a cursor. The guard covers create/update/delete, ordinary sync, full snapshot, and continuation pages, including a race with the first growth write. An old client must never silently skip growth data.
- A local save succeeds offline and queues sync. Failed sync leaves the local record and a visible retry state according to existing app behavior. A failed edit or delete retains the prior record and displays an error. No percentile result is cached as authoritative state; a missing or corrupt bundled table yields an unavailable-percentile state rather than an invented number.
- Deleting a local-only baby hides that baby and retains its growth profile and check-ins with the rest of the local care log. In a shared family, owner deletion tombstones the profile and check-ins in the same atomic server cascade as other entries. Owner export includes the raw growth profile and check-ins; calculated percentiles can be recomputed after restore and are not archived.

## Verification and release constraints

- Focused tests cover form validation; creation, edit, deletion, backdating, and same-day ordering; independent zero/one/two-point chart thresholds; missing legacy profile; corrected-age boundaries; lying/standing conversion; and golden WHO percentile examples. Test the rendered Insights and Growth screens, including accessibility and compact/Dynamic Type layout, rather than relying only on model tests.
- Migration tests open a pre-growth store and verify existing data survives. Sync tests cover two caregivers, offline replay, snapshot and journal parity, profile/check-in conflicts, child deletion, archive export, and older-client cursor rejection including continuation races. Run the applicable Swift, Go, protocol, build, and simulator UI gates once the implementation is assembled, then inspect the whole feature diff. Validate on an installed iPhone when available; report any device check that could not run.
- Localize new user-facing text in all supported app languages. Do not add custom types, clinical risk labels, alerts, or new growth standards in this release.
- Before distributing a bundle of WHO reference tables, identify the exact source files and applicable reuse terms, retain attribution, and obtain permission if the intended distribution requires it. WHO states that commercial reuse can require permission; if the rights cannot be confirmed, the percentile feature cannot ship with those bundled tables as designed. See [WHO copyright and permissions](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/copyright).
