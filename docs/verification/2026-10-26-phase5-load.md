| Pulls (`POST /v1/sync`, no mutations), open loop | 600/s | 1,200/s | plus hint pulls by live devices |
| Writes (1–3 mutations) | 10/s | 20/s | |
| Live sockets | 4,000 | 8,000 | idle except pings |

| Live Activity `PUT` | 0.5/s | 1/s | |

**Pull cursor mix:** 80% at the device's own cursor, 15% rewound 1–50 changes, 4% rewound 51–1,000, and 1% rewound to 0 and paged to the end.

**Write kind mix:** feed 33%, diaper 30%, sleep 20%, timer 7%, supplement 3%, measurement 2%, immunization 2%, pump 2%, growth profile 1%. The operations are create 85%, update 12% and delete 3%. 15% of write requests carry 2–3 mutations.

**Generator behaviour changed during Wave D, and the changes affect the counts:**
- A device's bearer requests wait for its own refresh, as iOS does (585c43bf, 8bf05115). This removed the generator-made `invalid_page_token` and `session_revoked` outcomes: s1w0a had 0 of each **[L]**.
- A live device leaves its old socket for the server to retire after a refresh, as iOS does (e83ea1df).
- Untracked devices read pull and snapshot pages without decoding the payloads (W4-J). The generator's own scheduler lag fell from 34–67 ms p99 on the Mac to 1.06 ms on the VM **[M]**.
- **A device whose refresh was lost in a restart renews before its next request** (293e4f12), as iOS's `prepareSession` does. Before it, the generator sent the stale access token and counted the 64 `access_expired` of v2 runs/5 against the server (section 7). Only the restart cell exercises it.

### Why 2.5x sheds: the stall root cause

Admission has 96 slots and no queue. At 2x–2.5x the node takes about 1,700–2,100 requests a second. Any DB stall longer than a few hundred milliseconds pushes in-flight work past 96, and `TryAcquire` sheds the rest as 503 `server_busy` (`admission/controller.go:33`) **[M/I]**. Four stall sources were found:

1. **`families` autovacuum invalidates the pull's plan in every backend at once.**
   - **[M]** Admission rejections cluster in the 5-s sample at about :40 past each minute. The phase drifts with the autovacuum launcher and survives app restarts and new generator runs.
   - **[M]** `families` was autovacuumed and autoanalyzed 201 times each in about 5.3 h.
   - **[I, arithmetic]** At 2x every write updates `families.next_sequence`. That is about 1,200 modifications a minute, against an analyze threshold of 1,050.
   - **[M]** The pull, a generic plan over every `care_entities` partition, was re-planned 53–85 times a minute at 2x and above. Mean plan time was 28–46 ms and the maximum 0.3–0.93 s. At 1x it was 32 a minute at 5.1 ms.
   - **[M]** These bursts account for all 231 rejections of the v2 ramp's 2x step and 829 of the 990 at 2.5x. Without them 2.5x would have been 0.031% (197 / 630,930) and passed.
   - **A/B (both at 8cedfbaf, 2.5x, 10 min, socket limit 20,000) [M]:**
     - avA (baseline autovacuum) passed at 0.0831% (1,049 / 1,262,681), with 806 pull plans.
     - avB turned `families` autovacuum off and `refresh_receipts` `vacuum_truncate` off. Pull plans fell to 67, which confirms the mechanism. But the pull's mean execution rose from 0.211 to 0.353 ms (unvacuumed `families`), and avB failed at 0.1808% on a different stall (item 2).
     - avB's table settings were an A/B arm only and were reset.
   - **Fix S1 (c63bd441):** 000037 takes `families` out of the pull's plan, and autovacuum stays on.
     - **[L]** s1w0a had 30 pull plans (avA 806) at 0.228 ms exec. s1w0b had 0 plans at 0.287 ms.
     - **The mechanism is gone, but the unexpected ratio did not improve.** s1w0a failed at 0.15% and s1w0b at 3.02%, both from other sources (items 2 and 3).
     - **[M: review]** Partition autovacuum can still re-plan the pull (DB-fix review M1).
2. **Dirty-buffer eviction storms.**
   - **[M: avB]** 15–49 backends sat in `IO/DataFileWrite` for 0.5–1 s, about 20–80 s after a sessions autovacuum ended. The bgwriter was at its cap: `maxwritten_clean` 2,894 in 601 s, against 3.6–4.6k buffer allocations a second.
   - b35a2bba raised `bgwriter_lru_maxpages` to 1000 and set `bgwriter_delay` to 50ms. The S1 arms ran with it.
   - **Its effect alone was not isolated** (no A/B arm without it).
3. **Storage-latency episodes from the Hyper-V host.**
   - **[M: soak20k vs runs/1]** Read time per block was 0.40 ms against 0.098, and WAL fsync 1.06 ms against 0.72. Rows, pages, blocks per call and plans were all flat.
   - Two stalls of about 30 s (22:37:30–22:38:00 and 22:46:10–22:46:36Z) account for about 33,000 rejections. In both, the whole pool sat in `WALWrite` and `DataFileRead`, and dozens of COMMITs finished together after 0.54–0.85 s.
   - Host load1 rose to 13–15 with no busy process.
   - s1w0b had `request_authority` block reads at 1.21 ms (normal about 0.1) **[L]**.
   - **[I]** The cause is outside PostgreSQL and outside the VM: the virtual disk or Windows host activity.
   - **Open question for the owner:** what did the Windows host do at 22:30–22:50Z on 2026-10-25?
4. **Pool connections expiring together.** pgxpool's default 1 h lifetime has no jitter.
   - **[M]** The maint cell lost 5,305 of its 5,542 `server_busy` in a 4-s stall at 19:42:55. That was exactly 1 h after the restart cell's reconnect burst, with 30 backends in `LockManager` while cold backends re-planned.
   - 56b266af spreads expiry over 15 minutes.
   - **Not re-measured:** no maint cell has run since.

### 2026-10-26: the generator fixes and the hub ping race

**The generator fixes (5b220da7).** The v4 2x soak (runs/1, b70b840b, wait 0) failed only on 2 unexpected live losses, and drew 1,355 `server_busy` in four bursts. All of it was generator-made **[M: vm-v4-soak-analysis]**:

**The hub ping race (3fef1381, 1daf9536): a server bug the load test found.** With the generator fixed, the losses that remained were real: a socket that the sweep retired with 1008 "authorization changed" could reach the client as 1013 "slow consumer", or with no close frame at all. Either way the client never got the 1008 that tells it to renew.
- **The mechanism, part one (3fef1381).**
  - The sweep releases a lease (cancelling its context) and sends 1008 from its own goroutine. Meanwhile the lease's `run` loop can take a ping tick: `select` picks among ready cases, so the ping can win over `ctx.Done()`.
  - The ping ran under the cancelled lease context and failed at once, and the loop then closed **1013 "slow consumer" over the sweep's 1008**. A ping cut off by the socket's lifetime also closed 1013, not 1000.
  - **Fix:** `lease.writeFailed` leaves a cancelled lease to its releaser's close (the sweep's, an invalidation's, a domain change's or shutdown's, each of which sends one), closes 1000 "maximum lifetime" when the lifetime ended it, and closes 1013 only when a write fails on its own.
- **The mechanism, part two (1daf9536).**
  - coder/websocket v1.8.15's `setupWriteTimeout` (`conn.go:171`) **closes the transport** via `context.AfterFunc` when a write's context ends.
  - The ping and hint writes ran under the lease's context. So a sweep releasing the lease cancelled the write in flight (or the next write started on a cancelled context), and **the TCP dropped under the 1008** the sweep was sending: the client saw no frame (code -1).
  - **Fix:** writes run under `context.WithoutCancel(lease)` with the hub's write timeout. The loop checks the lease's end before each write (`ended`) and after a failure (`writeFailed`): 1000 on the lifetime, otherwise the releaser's close.

#### What the verifier needs to anchor at the oldest kept partition

From `internal/authority/verifier.go:307` (`verifyScope`), `retentionAnchor` (`:467`) and `000035_audit_retention.sql`:

1. **One anchor row per chain that lost rows.** `audit_retention_anchors(scope, scope_id, anchor_seq, anchor_hash, anchored_at, dropped_before)` holds the seq and hash of the chain's last dropped row, which is also the first kept row's `prev_hash`, plus the end of the dropped partition. Only `app.drop_expired_audit_partition` writes it, and only forward. The runtime role can only `SELECT` it.
2. **The drop checks links before anchoring.** For every chain with rows in the expiring partition, the drop requires that the chain is not failed, that its head is at or past the new anchor, and that its rows from the previous anchor through the new one are exactly one per seq and linked by `prev_hash`. That covers rows in any partition. Row hashes are not recomputed. Otherwise it raises `XX001 'audit retention refused'` and changes nothing.
3. **`verifyScope` reads the anchor in its repeatable-read snapshot.** It reads the anchor alongside the head; verifier transactions lock the audit table first, so a drop cannot commit between them. It then walks from `anchor_seq + 1` with the running hash set to `anchor_hash`.
4. **What fails a chain.** The head behind the anchor fails it, as does an anchor or checkpoint at the same seq with another hash. So does any row at or below the anchor stamped at or after `dropped_before + 24h` (a row inserted after the drop; fix N1 of Task 27). A checkpoint below the anchor names an expired row and is ignored.

The verifier needs nothing more at this scale. Under (c) no chain moved during either window, so the incremental verifier had no work.
