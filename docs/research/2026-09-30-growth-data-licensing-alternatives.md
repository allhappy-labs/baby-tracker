# Growth data licensing and alternatives

Checked: 2026-09-30. Scope: free public iOS app, offline sex-specific weight, length/height and head-circumference references from birth through 60 months. Corrected age remains app logic. This is a source investigation, not a legal opinion or authorization to bundle data.

## Can the data have a different license from the app?

**Yes in principle; separate licensing does not itself establish permission for these WHO files.** CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO distinguishes a Collection of independent works from an Adaptation: sections 4(a) and 4(b) leave the rest of a Collection outside the data's license. An adaptation remains subject to ShareAlike. Converting formats is expressly permitted, but extracting selected columns, altering values, or integrating protected material requires a fact-specific classification. A separate resource directory and notices help document the boundary; they do not decide it. The same license prohibits additional legal restrictions and effective technological measures that restrict recipients' licensed rights. [CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO, sections 1–4](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/3.0/igo/legalcode.en).

**A zero price and public download are insufficient to establish noncommercial use.** The test concerns commercial advantage or monetary compensation, so the actual distribution/business context matters, including promotion, advertising and paid related services. A genuinely noncommercial use may qualify if the grant applies. [CC NonCommercial FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#does-my-use-violate-the-noncommercial-clause-of-the-licenses).

Individual facts and database rights are separate questions. CC explains that facts may lack copyright while selection/arrangement and, in some jurisdictions, substantial database extraction can be protected. This investigation does not establish that copying all WHO LMS tables is exempt worldwide. [CC database FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#which-components-of-databases-are-protected-by-copyright).

For App Store delivery, review the actual applicable EULA and distribution protections against the chosen data license. An attribution screen alone does not resolve restrictions on copying or adapting the data. CC specifically addresses DRM and platform terms; a separate unrestricted download should not be assumed to cure a restricted copy. No current Apple contract audit was performed. [CC DRM and platform FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#can-i-use-effective-technological-measures-such-as-drm-when-i-share-cc-licensed-material).

## WHO has several distinct licensing routes

| Artifact / terms | Evidence and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Six WHO expanded XLSX tables | WHO supplies girls/boys files for [weight](https://www.who.int/toolkits/child-growth-standards/standards/weight-for-age), [length/height](https://www.who.int/toolkits/child-growth-standards/standards/length-height-for-age), and [head circumference](https://www.who.int/toolkits/child-growth-standards/standards/head-circumference-for-age). They satisfy the intended indicator/sex coverage. Exact URLs and prior byte-level license-notice inspection are recorded in the [existing source investigation](2026-09-30-who-growth-reference-sources.md); this follow-up did not redownload or modify the workbooks. |
| Publication copyright policy | WHO permits reuse of material **issued under** CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO, and directs other materials/commercial uses to permissions. Its open-access policy says pre-2017 publications were not retrospectively reissued under that license. A workbook's recent modification date therefore cannot establish its license. [Copyright](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/copyright), [open access](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/open-access). |
| General WHO dataset terms — a relevant additional route | These cover machine-readable datasets published on `www.who.int`. Section 1 grants reproduction, extraction, distribution and inclusion in other products for public-health purposes. Section 4 permits minimal stylistic alterations of figures/tables, but requires written authorization for other modifications, including deletions. Other clauses require attribution, restrict commercial promotion, and allow termination with cessation/destruction of copies. **Inference:** potentially relevant to growth workbooks, but neither exact-file coverage nor permission for a reduced/transformed offline LMS bundle is established here. This is stronger evidence than saying WHO provides no reuse route; it is not a clean permissive grant. [WHO general dataset terms, sections 1–5 and 12](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/data-policy/terms-and-conditions). |
| `data.who.int` datasets | The portal's default is CC BY 4.0 plus additional binding terms and exclusions for third-party material. Its stated scope is datasets available through that portal. No matching growth-reference LMS dataset under these terms was established; do not transfer the portal license to separate CDN workbooks. [Portal terms](https://data.who.int/about/data/terms-and-conditions). |
| WHO Anthro PC/mobile software | The EULA expressly accompanies that software and related documentation. It allows attributed copying/distribution but excludes sale and commercial/promotional use, requires permission for modification/reverse engineering, asks derived-product developers to contact WHO, and prohibits using software contents in a product sold/licensed for a fee. Its text does not establish the license of separate XLSX downloads or the separate R package. [Anthro software EULA](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/child-growth/child-growth-standards/software/license2.pdf). |
| Official WHO `anthro` R package | [DESCRIPTION](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WorldHealthOrganization/anthro/master/DESCRIPTION) declares GPL-3. WHO itself distributes [growth-standard text files](https://github.com/WorldHealthOrganization/anthro/tree/master/data-raw/growthstandards), and the [build script](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WorldHealthOrganization/anthro/master/data-raw/growthstandards/growthstandards.R) incorporates weight, length and head-circumference tables as package data. This is substantial first-party evidence for a GPL package route, not merely a third-party mirror. No separate permissive data grant or explicit clarification of extracted-table licensing was established. The package grant must not be treated as a grant for the separate CDN workbooks. |

For the GPL route, [the package's GPLv3 text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WorldHealthOrganization/anthro/master/LICENSE.md), sections 5, 6 and 10, permits aggregates of independent works, requires GPL licensing of covered combined/derived works, governs corresponding source for non-source distribution, and bars further restrictions. Merely placing files separately does not prove aggregation; code copied/ported from the package and a separately read data file are materially different proposals. GPL permits commercial use and is not a noncommercial license. The [GNU aggregation FAQ](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.en.html#MereAggregation) explains the distinction. FSF has historically challenged App Store distribution over additional restrictions; its [2010 enforcement account](https://www.fsf.org/blogs/licensing/more-about-the-app-store-gpl-enforcement) is evidence of the issue, not a determination about today's terms or this data-only design. GPL is therefore a route to investigate, not an automatically easier iOS release route.

### Practical path: unchanged workbook resources

**Plausible, but not confirmed:** bundle the six original XLSX files unchanged, with a separately written runtime reader. This avoids distributing a reduced table with deleted columns and aligns more closely with section 1's express extraction/inclusion rights and section 4's limits on modification. Parsing into transient memory is distinct from distributing modified files. [WHO general dataset terms, sections 1 and 4](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/data-policy/terms-and-conditions).

Unresolved points under those same terms are:

- Whether datasets linked from `www.who.int` but served by `cdn.who.int` fall within the definition, and whether this personal growth-tracking product satisfies the public-health purpose.
- Whether calculated percentiles/z-scores are permitted derived presentations, rather than prohibited alterations; the definition expressly includes derived tables/charts, but does not specifically approve this calculation workflow.
- Commercial promotion restrictions; required WHO/country attribution versus section 7's restriction on WHO's name (ordinary attribution appears intended, but broad branding is not authorized).
- App Store redistribution and downstream terms, plus compliance with the cessation/destruction clause for already installed offline copies.

This would be the narrower proposal to present for clarification. Keeping original bytes reduces the transformation ambiguity; it does not settle these other conditions. [WHO general dataset terms, definition and sections 3–7, 12](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/data-policy/terms-and-conditions).

## Alternatives against first-release coverage

| Source | Coverage and offline suitability | Rights / change in clinical reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **CDC 2000 own LMS tables** | Boys/girls weight and recumbent length from birth–36 months; weight and stature from 24–240 months. These can cover weight and length/height through 60 months. **Head circumference ends at 36 months**, leaving 36–60 months uncovered. Published CSV/XLS tables are technically suitable for offline calculation. [CDC files and age conventions](https://www.cdc.gov/growthcharts/cdc-data-files.htm). | Best established alternative with fewer redistribution restrictions, but not a complete replacement. CDC's reuse policy generally permits reproduction without copyright permission, subject to attribution, prominent nonendorsement, unchanged substantive content, and notice that originals are free. It warns about third-party material and international copyright differences. [CDC policy](https://www.cdc.gov/other/agencymaterials.html). |
| **WHO files hosted by CDC** | CDC provides WHO LMS files; those remain WHO references, rather than an independently developed replacement. [CDC WHO data page](https://www.cdc.gov/growthcharts/who-data-files.htm). | Hosting on `cdc.gov` does not resolve upstream rights: CDC expressly excepts third-party copyrighted material. The CDC policy cannot safely be used to relabel these WHO data public domain. [CDC policy](https://www.cdc.gov/other/agencymaterials.html). |
| **RCPCH growth references / UK-WHO** | Repository supplies WHO, CDC and UK reference collections. They require selecting and validating the exact constituent dataset; UK-WHO is a composite reference. [RCPCH source library](https://github.com/rcpch/growth-references). | Its current README explicitly preserves upstream rights, restricts UK references to their MRC license, and applies CC BY-SA only to material RCPCH has rights to license. The repository's open-data description is not a WHO grant. The hosted API also changes the offline architecture. [RCPCH API description](https://www.rcpch.ac.uk/resources/growth-charts/digital/about). |
| **MIT `hms-dbmi/anthro` gem** | Author states it includes WHO and CDC data obtained from CDC. [Author's README](https://github.com/hms-dbmi/anthro). | **Permissively licensed software that mirrors upstream values:** its MIT statement does not demonstrate authority to relicense WHO data. No complete independent data-rights chain was established; do not use it to bypass WHO licensing. |
| **INTERGROWTH-21st** | Provides fetal, newborn and preterm standards; project follow-up extends to 2 years, not the requested general birth–60-month reference. Downloadable tables exist. [Project standards/tools](https://intergrowth21.tghn.org/standards-tools/). | Freely downloading/using charts is stated, but this page does not establish a broad app redistribution/adaptation license. Different population/age purpose and missing coverage make it unsuitable as the complete replacement. |

Choosing CDC 2000 changes the clinical reference. CDC recommends WHO for US children below 2 years and CDC thereafter; using CDC from birth would depart from that recommendation. Switching can change percentile/classification, including because of reference-population and length/height differences. These are product/clinical decisions, not an interchangeable data import. Keeping WHO below 2 years also keeps its rights question. [CDC recommendations and transition guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/growth-chart-training/hcp/using-growth-charts/who-using.html).

## Recommendation and unresolved questions

Preserve separate app/data licensing as the intended structure, without selecting a new app license. The evidence above supports that structure conditionally, but **this review did not establish a complete, clearly permissive alternative covering all three measures through 60 months**. CDC is the strongest partial alternative; it requires an explicit scope/reference decision for head circumference after 36 months and infant charts.

To retain WHO coverage, resolve the specific general-dataset or package-license route, or obtain permission via [WHO's official process](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/permissions). A concrete request would identify the six workbook URLs, exact extraction/format conversion, unchanged reference values, free public offline iOS distribution, attribution, business context, and separate app-code license. It should ask about App Store restrictions and downstream redistribution, rather than merely permission to view the charts. No request was sent, no data bundled, and no app license changed.
