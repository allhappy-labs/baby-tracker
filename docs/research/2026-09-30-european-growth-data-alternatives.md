# European growth-reference data alternatives

Research date: 30 September 2026. Scope: offline, sex-specific percentiles for weight, length/height, and head circumference from birth through at least age five, in a free public Baby Tracker app. The user will **not pay for a dataset or data licence**. The app can calculate corrected age itself; the reference must support that age. This is a source and reuse-term inventory, not a legal clearance or clinical recommendation.

## Finding

I found **no verified European authority dataset that both covers all three measures through age five in numerical form and expressly permits its extraction and redistribution in this app**. There are two useful leads:

1. **Flanders 2024** is the closest clinical match. The Flemish government agency Opgroeien publishes separate girls' and boys' 0–5-year charts for weight, length, and head circumference, built from Kind en Gezin measurements. Its [official project page](https://www.opgroeien.be/kennis/cijfers-en-onderzoek/samenwerking-onderzoek/onderzoeksproject-nieuwe-vlaamse-groeicurven) says software integrators can request technical data from the research contacts listed there. It also lists an existing paid iOS app using the February 2024 dataset; that app's price says nothing about the price of access to the data. The page does **not** grant public redistribution rights, state whether technical data are free, or provide a numerical LMS download. This source qualifies only if the technical data and required redistribution permission are available **at no charge**, with the data separately licensed from the app if needed.
2. **Switzerland 2025/2026** has a genuinely promising open partial source. The [PEZZ study](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/14/16/5912) publishes supplementary numerical percentiles and model parameters for girls' height and weight from birth to 18 years and boys' from birth to 20 years (Tables S1, S2, S5, S6). The [article is CC BY 4.0](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12387566/), and [MDPI says its article licence covers supplementary material and data](https://www.mdpi.com/openaccess). [Pädiatrie schweiz adopted the new reference in June 2026](https://www.paediatrieschweiz.ch/news/neue-wachstumskurven/). **Head circumference is not in those tables**: the society's chart uses Zurich longitudinal data at 0–2 years and German KiGGS data at 2–18 years. The society's [combined chart PDF](https://files.paediatrieschweiz.ch/production/uploads/2026/07/PCH_Wachstumskurven_260701_DE.pdf) requires attribution and forbids changing its curves without consent. The PEZZ article licence should not be assumed to license that separate head-reference material or the society's combined PDF. I did not independently download and validate the supplementary ZIP, so the exact parameter encoding and interpolation still need technical verification before implementation.

## Product and rights implications

- The European alternatives do not automatically avoid the WHO issue: the UK-WHO reference embeds WHO 2006, while Norway and Denmark recommend WHO for young children. [Swiss 2026](https://www.paediatrieschweiz.ch/news/neue-wachstumskurven/) uses WHO for BMI under age two, but its separate weight/height series are national. Keep provenance **per metric and age band**.
- Replacing WHO with a national reference changes what a percentile means. The [Swiss society](https://www.paediatrieschweiz.ch/news/neue-wachstumskurven/) describes its new curves as population references, and the [Norwegian authority](https://www.helsedirektoratet.no/retningslinjer/helsestasjons-og-skolehelsetjenesten/helsestasjon-05-ar/veiing-og-maling) distinguishes these from the prescriptive WHO standard. Label source and population in the UI; do not silently switch a child's existing percentile history.
- The best next outreach, if a single 0–5 European dataset is desired, is the two [Opgroeien contacts](https://www.opgroeien.be/kennis/cijfers-en-onderzoek/samenwerking-onderzoek/onderzoeksproject-nieuwe-vlaamse-groeicurven). Ask first whether the exact numerical files and offline Apple App Store redistribution rights are **free of charge**; if so, ask about attribution, transformation to compact tables, and licensing the data separately from app code. A paid licence is outside the agreed scope. This report does not authorize contacting them.
- If using Swiss article tables for weight/height, identify a separately permitted head reference or agree on a shorter head coverage before coding. The [Swiss society's PDF restriction](https://files.paediatrieschweiz.ch/production/uploads/2026/07/PCH_Wachstumskurven_260701_DE.pdf) and [RKI repository restriction](https://www.rki.de/DE/Institut/Service-und-Besucherinformation/Bibliothek/edoc_nutzerhinweise.html) make simply extracting their head curves unsuitable as a confirmed rights path.

The general [European Commission open-data guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/open-data-and-high-value-datasets-step-step-access-guide) says reuse terms belong to each dataset/provider. [Eurostat's permissive default](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/help/copyright-notice) applies to its own publications with exceptions; it does not relicense national, WHO, or third-party growth data.
